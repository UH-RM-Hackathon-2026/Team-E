## 1. Problem

Each respondent answered a survey about their flu concern and knowledge, protective behaviours, doctor recommendations, opinions on the vaccine, and demographics. The task is to predict whether they received the seasonal flu vaccine.

Instead of judging models only on accuracy, we ranked them by **estimated total financial cost**, using:

| Cost item | Value |
|---|---|
| Vaccine, per person | $300 |
| Hospitalisation, per person | $18,500 |

## 5. Methodology

### 5.1 Dataset exploration
- Checked the shape, data types, missing values and duplicates of both datasets.
- Plotted the target distribution and produced summary statistics for every column.

### 5.2 Exploratory data analysis
Vaccination rates were compared across three key characteristics:

- **Age group**: uptake rises steadily with age, from about 35% for 18–34-year-olds to about 73% for those aged 65+.
- **Doctor recommendation**: respondents whose doctor recommended the vaccine are much more likely to be vaccinated.
- **Perceived vaccine effectiveness**: belief that the vaccine works is strongly linked to uptake.

### 5.3 Preprocessing
- Dropped `respondent_id`, a unique identifier with no predictive value.
- Split the data **80% training and 20% validation**, stratified on the target (3,804 / 952 rows, `random_state=42`).
- Built a scikit-learn `ColumnTransformer` pipeline:
  - **Numerical columns**: median imputation, then `StandardScaler`.
  - **Categorical columns**: most-frequent imputation, then `OneHotEncoder(handle_unknown="ignore")`.
- The preprocessing sits inside each model pipeline, so it is fitted on the training data only and applied identically to the validation and test data.

### 5.4 Models
Five classifiers were trained with the same preprocessing:

| Model | Settings |
|---|---|
| Logistic Regression | `max_iter=1000` |
| Random Forest | 100 trees |
| Gradient Boosting | 100 estimators |
| Extra Trees | 100 trees |
| HistGradientBoosting | defaults |

### 5.5 Evaluation
Each model was evaluated on the validation set with accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, ROC-AUC and a confusion matrix.

### 5.6 Financial cost analysis
The dataset has no hospitalisation outcomes, so we estimated them with **illustrative hospitalisation probabilities**:

| Risk group | Vaccinated | Not vaccinated |
|---|---|---|
| High risk (age 65+ **or** ≥1 chronic condition) | 3% | 10% |
| Lower risk | 1% | 3% |

For each model on the validation set:

- **Vaccine cost**: $300 × the number of people predicted to accept the vaccine.
- **Compensation cost**: $300 × the number of willing people the model missed (actual = 1, predicted = 0).
- **Hospitalisation cost**: $18,500 × the sum of each person's hospitalisation probability. People count as vaccinated only if they were willing and the model predicted them correctly.
- **Total cost** = vaccine cost + compensation cost + hospitalisation cost.

## 6. Results

Results on the validation set (952 respondents), ranked by total estimated cost:

| Rank | Model | Accuracy | Precision | Recall | F1 | ROC-AUC | Total cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Logistic Regression** | 0.7847 | 0.8024 | 0.7945 | 0.7984 | 0.8480 | **$872,610** |
| 2 | Gradient Boosting | 0.7815 | 0.7942 | 0.8004 | 0.7973 | **0.8534** | $873,300 |
| 3 | HistGradientBoosting | 0.7794 | 0.7911 | 0.8004 | 0.7957 | 0.8450 | $875,750 |
| 4 | Random Forest | 0.7647 | 0.7754 | 0.7906 | 0.7829 | 0.8377 | $878,450 |
| 5 | Extra Trees | 0.7574 | 0.7789 | 0.7652 | 0.7720 | 0.8244 | $888,860 |

**Cost breakdown:**

| Model | Vaccine | Compensation | Hospitalisation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Logistic Regression | $151,800 | $31,500 | $689,310 |
| Gradient Boosting | $154,500 | $30,600 | $688,200 |
| HistGradientBoosting | $155,100 | $30,600 | $690,050 |
| Random Forest | $156,300 | $32,100 | $690,050 |
| Extra Trees | $150,600 | $36,000 | $702,260 |

**Key findings:**

- **Logistic Regression** had the lowest estimated total cost. **Gradient Boosting** had the best ROC-AUC.
- The top three models are within about $3,000 (0.4%) of each other, so the more complex models gave no clear financial advantage.
- Hospitalisation is about 79% of total cost in every model, so the ranking is driven mostly by how well each model identifies people who will accept the vaccine.

## 8. Limitations

- **Assumed hospitalisation risks.** The probabilities are illustrative assumptions, not observed outcomes, so the cost figures are scenario-based. Different assumptions could change the ranking.
- **Small cost differences.** The gaps between the top models are small and may not hold on the unseen test set.
- **Single validation split.** Results come from one 80/20 split, without cross-validation, so they include some random variation.
- **Missing values.** These were imputed with the median or most frequent value, which may hide useful information in the fact that an answer is missing.
- **Simplified vaccine allocation.** The model of who gets a vaccine is simplified and does not estimate individual treatment effects.

## 9. Future work

- Hyperparameter tuning and k-fold cross-validation to measure how much the results vary.
- Sensitivity analysis over a range of hospitalisation probabilities, to test whether the model ranking stays the same.
- Choosing the decision threshold to minimise cost directly, instead of using the default 0.5.
- Explainable AI (SHAP values, permutation importance) to identify the main drivers of vaccination.
